## 9. Please describe your model/approach in detail.

Two independent routes share normalisation (bcftools 1.24) and annotation (Ensembl VEP 116.0d85231 with MANE Select transcripts and gnomAD v4.1 frequencies, ClinVar release 2026-10-04, SpliceAI 1.3.1) and then rank separately. Route A: Exomiser 15.1.0 with data release 2602 ranks every gene in the genome from the child's 8 HPO terms, with the exome and exome-plus-introns presets, and makes no assumption about the diagnosis. Route B scores every variant, coding and non-coding, PASS and non-PASS, in 13 MVA and spindle-checkpoint genes with a points system adapted from the Bayesian ACMG/AMP framework, adds a known upstream hypomorph, pairs alleles within each gene, scores a pair by its weaker allele and excludes pairs proven to be in cis by read-backed phasing. Its weights, thresholds and tie-breaks were committed to git before the first ranking run and have not changed since. Code merges both routes into the 10-row CSV with a fixed EPCR ladder and checks it with the official scorer. Phase 2 adds per-allele ACMG/AMP evidence, a pre-registered sensitivity analysis, a mosaic-aneuploidy scan of the child's genome and a pre-registered benchmark on public genomes in which only the gene list changes between diseases. Full detail is in the report sections Design, Pipeline, Robustness, Aneuploidy and Scalability.

## 18. Please provide an estimate of run time and cost for your approach (if possible).

Measured on Apple M5, 10 cores (4 performance), 16 GB memory, macOS 26.5. One-time setup: download of about 91 GB of public resources listed in answer 14, which took 8.4 hours on this connection. Per genome: data download under 1 min; QC and sex check under 1 min; normalisation and annotation 19 min; Exomiser (two presets) 29 min; route B ranking under 1 min; CSV and self-check under 1 min; about 49 minutes in all, most of it Exomiser and annotation. Genomes are processed independently, so a cohort scales linearly and parallelises across machines. No AI model is part of the pipeline.

## 19. Provide a method abstract (up to 500 words). Please include strengths and limitations of your method as well as methodological detail.

Approach. We ranked candidate variants for a child with mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA) along two routes that share normalisation and annotation but not assumptions. Route A runs Exomiser genome-wide from the child's phenotype terms. Route B scores every variant in 13 MVA and spindle-checkpoint genes with a points system adapted from the Bayesian ACMG/AMP framework, keeps non-coding and non-PASS calls, adds a known upstream hypomorph, pairs alleles within each gene, scores a pair by its weaker allele and excludes pairs proven to be in cis. Every weight, threshold and tie-break was committed to git before the first ranking run, and every later commit is listed from git. Code merges the routes into the submission and checks it with the official scorer.

Result. Route B ranked first a BUB1B genotype made of a truncating allele and a missense allele, the configuration of most published MVA1 families. Route A (Exomiser) also ranked BUB1B first in both presets. Under ClinGen-refined ACMG/AMP criteria the truncating allele classifies as pathogenic; the missense allele remains of uncertain significance until parental testing confirms trans phase and a protein assay is done.

Robustness. A pre-registered sensitivity analysis changed one rule at a time in 48 scenarios; the rank-1 pair stayed first in 48. A pre-registered scan of the child's genome for mosaic whole-chromosome aneuploidy was reported, with its detection limit stated.

Scalability. The ranking engine and the disease knowledge are separate files: another recessive disease needs a new gene list, which one command builds from a PanelApp panel, not new code. A pre-registered benchmark placed ClinVar-pathogenic biallelic genotypes into 7 public genomes (GIAB) and re-ran route B, unchanged except for the gene list, under the MVA rules and 7 PanelApp disease panels (1 to 1,513 green genes). With ClinVar evidence removed, as for newly found variants, the placed genotype ranked first in 87% of 840 runs (MVA rules: 100%) and in the top five in 98%; with ClinVar evidence, first in 97%. By panel size: first in 99% for panels of up to 30 green genes and in 75% for panels above 100. One genome takes about 49 minutes on a laptop, without cloud or paid services, and genomes run independently, so a cohort scales linearly.

Strengths. Pre-registration with an audit trail; two independent routes; recessive-aware pairing with cis exclusion; non-coding and non-PASS variants in scope; per-allele evidence on calibrated scales; generalisation tested on public genomes with only the gene list changed; laptop-scale run time; 45 automated tests.

Limitations. Phase is inferred, not observed, so trans confirmation needs the parents. Route B depends on a correct gene list, and with large panels background candidates compete, which is why it is paired with the gene-agnostic route A. The benchmark uses public calls with synthetic call metrics and no SpliceAI. Mobile-element insertions, distant regulatory variants and low-level mosaic variants are largely invisible in a short-read germline VCF.
