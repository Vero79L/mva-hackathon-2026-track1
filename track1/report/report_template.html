Summary

We analysed the organiser-provided germline genome VCF of PROBAND01, a child living with mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA), along two routes that share annotation but not assumptions. Both put the same gene first.

Route A ranks every gene in the genome with Exomiser 15.1.0 from the child's phenotype terms and makes no assumption about which gene is involved. Route B scores every variant in 13 MVA and spindle-assembly-checkpoint genes, coding and non-coding, PASS and non-PASS, with a points system adapted from the Bayesian form of the ACMG/AMP guidelines. It then pairs alleles within each gene and excludes pairs proven to sit on the same chromosome. Its rules were committed to git before any ranking was run and have not changed since.

The top pair joins a nonsense allele, p.(Leu737Ter), with a missense allele, p.(Asn1002Lys), in the BUBR1 pseudokinase domain.

  • The nonsense allele sits 746 nucleotides upstream of the last exon junction, so the transcript is predicted to undergo nonsense-mediated decay.
  • The missense allele lies in the pseudokinase domain. The causal MVA1 missense variants studied in and near this domain lower BUBR1 levels, at least in part by destabilising the protein.

The pair reproduces the configuration seen in most published MVA1 families, one truncating and one missense allele.

Under ClinGen-refined ACMG/AMP criteria, the nonsense allele classifies as pathogenic. The missense allele stays a variant of uncertain significance under conservative settings, and becomes likely pathogenic once two things are added: parental testing to confirm trans phase, and a BUBR1 protein-level assay. Section sets out that path.

Mosaic variegated aneuploidy and BUBR1

The syndrome

MVA is a rare autosomal recessive disorder. Its defining finding is mosaic aneuploidy: trisomies and monosomies of many different chromosomes, in many tissues, with the proportion of aneuploid cells usually above 25%. Orphanet uses a lower threshold of more than 10%. Most patients also show premature chromatid separation (PCS), in which the sister chromatids of every chromosome appear separated and splayed in metaphase.

Severe intrauterine growth restriction and microcephaly are typical. Across about 31 published patients of mixed genetic cause, the most common features were:

  • growth retardation, 93.5%
  • microcephaly, 87%
  • developmental delay or intellectual disability, 77.4%
  • seizures, 45.1%
  • Dandy–Walker malformation, 25.8%
  • malignancy, 38.7%

Kajii and colleagues described five infants with PCS-MVA and reviewed five earlier reports. All ten had severe microcephaly, pre- and postnatal growth retardation with profound developmental delay, and a posterior fossa malformation such as the Dandy–Walker complex. Seven developed proven or probable Wilms tumour, bilateral in four.

BUBR1 in the spindle assembly checkpoint

BUB1B encodes BUBR1, a 1,050-residue protein of the spindle assembly checkpoint. Until every kinetochore is attached to the spindle, BUBR1 joins MAD2, BUB3 and CDC20 in the mitotic checkpoint complex, which holds the anaphase-promoting complex (APC/C) inactive. Its N-terminal half carries the parts needed for that inhibition:

  • two KEN boxes (residues 26–28 and 304–306)
  • a D-box (224–232)
  • three ABBA motifs (272–277, 340–345 and 528–533)
  • the BUB3-binding GLEBS motif (362–431), which brings it to kinetochores

A kinetochore attachment regulatory domain (KARD, about residues 665–682) contains an LxxIxE motif (669–674). Once primed by Plk1 phosphorylation at S676 and T680, this motif docks the PP2A-B56 phosphatase and stabilises kinetochore–microtubule attachments.

The C-terminal domain (766–1050) resembles a kinase. It keeps the catalytic triad, but catalysis is dispensable for accurate segregation. Instead, the residues that would contact ATP in a true kinase are needed for the protein's conformational stability. That point matters for this case, because the child's missense allele lies in this domain.

How MVA1 alleles behave

In the original families, each child carried one missense allele and one allele that truncated the protein or gave no transcript. Work on patient cells explains the pattern:

  • Full-length BUBR1 in four patient cell lines was reduced 2- to 6-fold.
  • Transcripts from truncating alleles (386X, 731X) were absent, and no truncated protein (386X, 731X, 753X) could be detected, consistent with nonsense-mediated decay.
  • Expressed in HeLa and U2OS cells, the MVA missense mutants in or near the kinase domain reached 5- to 10-fold lower levels than wild type. I909T and R727C turned over about twice as fast.
  • I909T and L1012P levels fell sharply when HSP90 was inhibited. The authors concluded that BUBR1 is destabilised by all kinase-domain-localised MVA mutations.
  • One patient allele carries two substitutions. Tested separately, L844F was poorly expressed and could not restore any defect, whereas Q921H was indistinguishable from wild type. Not every substitution in the domain is damaging.

A recurrent truncating allele right next to codon 737, c.2211insGTTA (p.Ser738fs), makes no detectable protein. Mice carrying one copy have less BubR1 and a shortened life- and healthspan.

MVA1 can also arise from hypomorphic alleles that coding-region analysis misses:

  • In Japanese PCS syndrome, one truncating allele is paired with an intergenic G>A variant 44 kb upstream of the gene (rs576524605), which lowers BUB1B transcription.
  • An Alu insertion in intron 8 caused exon skipping.
  • A homozygous intronic variant, c.2386-11A>G, was reported with PCS and gastrointestinal neoplasia.

Route B was built to catch alleles of these kinds, not just coding ones.

What Track 1 asks, and how this report answers it

Track 1 asks which variants cause the child's condition. A CSV of at most ten ranked rows is scored automatically, by rank points and by F-max over the submitted confidence values. The scoring is adapted from the CAGI6 Rare Genomes Project challenge. Separately, a judging panel reviews the methods for scientific rigour and depth of understanding.

The diagnosis of MVA is part of the public case description, and BUB1B is its most common cause. A gene-level prior was therefore unavoidable. We dealt with that in three ways:

  1. A gene-agnostic genome-wide route runs in parallel.
  2. The rules of the gene-focused route were frozen before any result existed.
  3. This report separates what each piece of evidence shows from what it does not.

Data and quality control

The organisers supplied, for a single sample (PROBAND01):

  • a germline genome VCF called with Sentieon 202308.02 and filtered with GATK VariantFiltration, on GRCh38 (no-alt analysis set with hs38d1 decoys, masked GRC exclusions v2), with chromosome names lacking the chr prefix
  • the raw reads (FASTQ)
  • a phenotype description in HPO terms ( terms)

Parental genomes are not provided. The organisers have stated that confirming phase would amount to confirming the answer. In the VCF, the per-sample INFO allele frequency is a within-sample fraction, not a population frequency, so population frequencies come only from gnomAD through VEP. Phase 1 used the VCF only; the reads were not re-aligned.

Genome-wide quality summary (aggregates only), computed with bcftools .

A sex check used the heterozygous-to-homozygous-alternate ratio on non-pseudoautosomal chrX and the count of chrY alternate calls (rule: male if ratio < 0.5 and at least 1,000 chrY calls; female if ratio ≥ 0.8 and fewer than 500). Its result set Exomiser's sex parameter. It is not reported here, because this report is published under CC BY 4.0 and the organisers' public description of the child does not include it.

Before annotation, the main contigs of the reference FASTA were checked against the VCF header for identical names and lengths, so that left-normalisation used exactly the sequence the variants were called on.

The HPO terms were checked against hp.obo ; obsolete identifiers were replaced by their successors. They were given to Exomiser unchanged. The terms themselves are not listed: they are part of the controlled dataset, and this report is public.

Design and pre-registration

Three rules shaped the analysis: two routes that do not depend on each other, ranking rules frozen before any result existed, and a submission assembled by code rather than by hand.

Route B makes many small choices: point weights, frequency thresholds, how to pair alleles, how to break ties. Each is defensible, and each could be adjusted after looking at the output to favour a preferred answer. That is the analytic flexibility that pre-registration exists to remove. We therefore wrote every rule into one configuration file, config/ranking_rules.yml, and committed it to git before the first ranking run.

Two scripts enforce the freeze:

  • 07_rank.sh and run_phase1.sh refuse to run if the file is uncommitted or carries uncommitted edits.
  • Every ranking run writes the rules commit, version and SHA-256 to a run manifest.

What was fixed in advance

  • Genes. Gene tiers and old gene-name aliases.
  • Known alleles. A single entry: the literature hypomorph 44 kb upstream of BUB1B.
  • Thresholds. Frequency limits, SpliceAI thresholds, and quality thresholds.
  • Scoring and pairing. Eleven point weights, the definition of a qualifying allele, pair scoring and cis exclusion, and the tie-break order.
  • Submission. The merge order of the ten rows and the EPCR values.

The full file is reproduced in Appendix . Route B's code (rank_panel.py, t1common.py) has the same commit as the rules and has not changed since.

What it does not show

  • That route B would generalise to other diseases. Its panel is disease-specific by design.
  • That the variants are causal. That needs the evidence in Section and the confirmations in Section .

The gene-agnostic check on the first point is route A, which knows nothing about the MVA panel. The single known allele in the rules is a literature allele, not either of the alleles that ended up ranked first.

Pipeline

Every step is a script in track1/scripts/. Finished steps whose inputs have not changed are skipped on re-runs. Versions of every tool are recorded in track1/env/ ().

Normalisation

The official scorer compares POS, REF and ALT literally, so representation decides whether a correct answer earns points. We used bcftools in this order:

  1. Stored each record's original CHROM:POS:REF:ALT in the ID field, so that every allele can be mapped back to the organisers' representation for submission.
  2. Split multi-allelic sites into biallelic records (norm -m -any).
  3. Left-aligned and normalised indels against the matching reference, checking REF bases (-f, -c w).
  4. Removed spanning-deletion (*) alleles.
  5. Kept the primary contigs.

Two alleles split from the same 1/2 site share an original ID, which proves they are on different chromosomes.

Annotation

  • VEP. Ensembl VEP ran fully offline with the matching cache (--everything --hgvsg), reporting MANE Select transcripts first and gnomAD v4.1 frequencies.
  • ClinVar. The release of was added as a custom annotation, so that its review status is available per allele.
  • SpliceAI. SpliceAI 1.3.1 scored every panel variant within ±500 bp, taking the largest of the four delta scores. A score counts only for the gene whose transcript it was computed on, so a splice prediction for a neighbouring gene cannot add points to BUB1B. The supporting threshold of 0.2 matches the ClinGen recommendation for PP3.
  • Structural variants. Structural-variant and copy-number calls overlapping the panel were extracted for manual review.

Route A: genome-wide, phenotype-driven

A GA4GH phenopacket was built from the HPO terms, the inferred sex and the VCF sample. Exomiser (data release ) ran twice:

  • with the default exome preset
  • with the preset that also scores intronic variants

Both runs considered every mode of inheritance. Exomiser combines a variant score (frequency and predicted pathogenicity) with a phenotype score that compares the patient's terms with human disease, mouse and fish annotations.

In the 100,000 Genomes Project pilot, it ranked the diagnostic variant first in 77% of solved cases, and in the top five in 88%. Without phenotype terms, top-1 performance in exomes fell from 74% to 3%.

The exome presets analyse PASS calls only and drop most non-coding variants. Those are the places where a hypomorphic second MVA allele can hide, and the reason route B exists.

Route B: pre-registered panel scoring

The panel comprises:

  • Tier A, six established MVA genes: BUB1B, CEP57, TRIP13, CENATAC, SLF2, SMC5.
  • Tier B, seven checkpoint and kinetochore genes with MVA-like reports: BUB1, BUB3, MAD1L1, MAD2L1BP, CEP192, KNL1, ZWINT.

Each gene is padded by 10 kb from the Ensembl 116 annotation, and the known upstream allele is added. All variants in these regions are scored, including non-PASS and non-coding ones; quality is used only for tie-breaks, because a real second allele can sit in a hard-to-call region.

An allele qualifies when all three of the following hold:

  • It is rare (maximum population frequency ≤ 1%), or listed as ClinVar P/LP, or a known allele.
  • It carries at least one functional item of evidence. PM2 and PP4 alone do not count as functional.
  • Its genotype is heterozygous or homozygous.

Within each gene, qualifying alleles are paired:

  • A homozygous allele pairs with itself.
  • Two heterozygous alleles form a pair whose score is that of the weaker allele, because a recessive diagnosis needs both alleles to hold.
  • Pairs proven to be in cis by read-backed phasing (shared PID, same PGT) are excluded.
  • A tier A gene with a single qualifying allele enters at half its score, below any true pair.

Ties are broken in a fixed order:

  1. the weaker allele's VEP impact
  2. phase confirmed in trans
  3. total points
  4. call quality
  5. lower allele frequency

From two rankings to ten rows

The submission is assembled by make_submission.py in a fixed order:

  1. Row 1: route B's best tier A pair.
  2. Rows 2–4: the next pairs in the same gene, which hedge against the wrong second allele.
  3. Rows 5–8: route A's best recessive candidates in other genes, written back to the organisers' VCF representation.
  4. Rows 9–10: reserved for manually reviewed secondary findings. None were submitted.

Rank points depend only on where the true answer appears: 100 for row 1, 50 for rows 2–3, 25 for rows 4–5, 10 for rows 6–10, and half of that if only one of a compound-heterozygous pair is listed. F-max is computed per variant over the submitted EPCR thresholds. The EPCR ladder (0.95, 0.60, 0.45, 0.35, 0.25, 0.18, 0.12, 0.08) is strictly decreasing, so every threshold adds exactly one row, and the best threshold can isolate a correct row.

Before upload, three checks ran:

  • Format. Header, ten rows at most, EPCR within (0, 1] and unique, chr prefix, proband identifier.
  • VCF lookup. Every allele must exist with the same REF and ALT at its position in the original VCF.
  • Scoring. The CSV was scored by an internal re-implementation of the metric and by the organisers' evaluation.py, imported unchanged.

Evidence for each allele

Each allele was assessed against the ClinGen refinements of the ACMG/AMP criteria, using the transcript and protein data below. The goal was not to make the pair look strong, but to state exactly how strong it is.

Allele 1, nonsense p.(Leu737Ter)

Identity and location. On the MANE Select transcript (NM_001211.6, ENST00000287598.11, 23 exons), codon 737 is TTA at c.2209–2211 in exon 17. Only two single-nucleotide changes turn it into a stop: c.2210T>A (TAA) and c.2210T>G (TGA), both at GRCh38 15:40,209,701. The child's allele is . Its call was , genotype , depth , allele depths , and genotype quality .

Nonsense-mediated decay. The last exon–exon junction is at c.2957/2958, so the new stop lies 746–749 nucleotides upstream of it, far beyond the 50–55-nucleotide boundary. It also passes every escape rule of the NMDetective decision tree:

  • It is not in the last exon or the last 50 nucleotides of the penultimate one.
  • It is not within 150 nucleotides of the start codon.
  • Exon 17 is 141 nucleotides long, well under the 407-nucleotide long-exon escape.

NMD is therefore predicted, and the prediction matches what has been observed a few codons away. In patient cells, the truncating alleles 731X and 753X in exon 17 make no detectable truncated protein, and the 731X transcript is absent. The recurrent c.2211insGTTA allele at codon 738 likewise makes no detectable protein. Even if a protein escaped decay, it would end before residue 766 and lack the entire pseudokinase domain.

Population and databases.

Criteria. PVS1 at very strong strength, following the ClinGen decision tree for a nonsense variant predicted to undergo NMD in a gene where loss of function causes disease. PM2_Supporting, applied at supporting strength as ClinGen now requires, since the allele is . PP4, at supporting strength, because MVA is a highly specific phenotype for which BUB1B is the predominant cause. PM3 cannot yet be used, because the other allele is not yet classified pathogenic. The total is 10 points, which classifies the allele as pathogenic; it would be likely pathogenic (9 points) without PP4.

Allele 2, missense p.(Asn1002Lys)

Identity and location. Codon 1002 is AAT at c.3004–3006 in exon 23, the last exon. Asparagine becomes lysine through c.3006T>A (AAA) or c.3006T>G (AAG), at GRCh38 15:40,220,612. The child's allele is . Its call was , genotype , depth , allele depths , and genotype quality .

Structural context. Residue 1002 lies inside the pseudokinase domain (766–1050), within the region annotated as a kinase fold by Gene3D and CDD, and in a part of BUBR1 with no predicted disorder after residue 720. The local sequence is GELWNKFFVRILNANDEATVSVLGEL. Two published landmarks lie close by:

  • the Plk1 phosphosite T1008, 6 residues away
  • L1012, 10 residues away. Its substitution to proline is an MVA1 allele whose product depends on HSP90 to fold and is lost when HSP90 is inhibited.

Because this domain's role is to hold BUBR1 in a stable conformation, not to catalyse, protein destabilisation is the mechanism to expect. It is the mechanism shown for the causal MVA1 missense variants in and near the domain: R727C and I909T turn over twice as fast as wild type, I909T and L1012P are lost when HSP90 is inhibited, and L844F is poorly expressed. The same study is also a caution. Q921H, which shares an allele with L844F, behaved like wild type, so a substitution in this domain is not damaging by default. That is why we treat p.(Asn1002Lys) as a variant to be tested, not a variant assumed to be pathogenic.

We read the computational evidence conservatively. ClinGen requires the tool for PP3 to be chosen before scores are seen. Our pre-registered tool was the SIFT and PolyPhen-2 consensus, which is not calibrated. For a calibrated tool we would have defaulted to REVEL, and REVEL is indeterminate here. We therefore do not count PP3 in the primary classification. AlphaMissense's PP3_Moderate is reported as a sensitivity result.

The AlphaMissense score also needs care in this domain. Two published MVA1 alleles with experimental defects score as likely benign: R727C (0.238), which turns over twice as fast as wild type, and L844F (0.292), which is poorly expressed. L1012P scores 0.872. AlphaMissense therefore misses some destabilising variants here, so a high score supports, but does not settle, the question.

Population and databases.

Criteria. PM2_Supporting (1 point). PM3 at supporting strength (1 point): it is observed with a pathogenic allele in the same gene, phase unknown, which earns 0.5 points in the ClinGen PM3 scheme. PP4 (1 point). The total is 3 points, a variant of uncertain significance, and 5 points if AlphaMissense's PP3_Moderate is admitted. It reaches likely pathogenic (6 points or more) with any two of three steps:

  • parental testing that confirms trans, lifting PM3 to moderate
  • a functional assay showing reduced BUBR1 in the child's cells, PS3 at moderate strength or higher
  • a calibrated in-silico call made by a pre-specified tool

Phase

The two alleles are 10,911 bp apart (15:40,209,701 and 15:40,220,612). That is far beyond what 150-bp paired reads can link, and the VCF carries . Parental data are withheld by design, so phase is unobserved, and route B treated the pair as unphased.

The case for trans is biological, not statistical:

  • two rare, damaging alleles in the predominant MVA gene
  • a child with the matching recessive phenotype
  • the same allele configuration as published families

If the two were in cis, the child would carry one normal BUB1B copy, and the disease would remain unexplained by this gene. Testing the parents resolves the question in one step, and it is the first confirmation we recommend.

The upstream hypomorphic allele

Route B's single pre-registered known allele was the intergenic G>A variant 44 kb upstream of BUB1B (GRCh38 15:40,117,088, rs576524605). It lowers transcription and, paired with a truncating allele, causes PCS-MVA in Japanese families. Its allele frequency is about 0.1% in East Asian populations and 0.02% overall.

Route A's other candidates

Rows 5–8 of the submission carry route A's best recessive candidates outside the MVA panel, at low confidence (EPCR 0.25 to 0.08). They are a hedge against the panel's prior, not a competing diagnosis.

    Fanconi anaemia deserves a sentence of its own, because it shares chromosomal instability, growth failure and microcephaly with MVA. It is diagnosed by chromosome breakage after diepoxybutane or mitomycin C, whereas MVA is defined by numerical aneuploidy and premature chromatid separation. A breakage test and a karyotype therefore separate the two cleanly. Other microcephalic chromosomal-instability syndromes are distinguishable the same way:

    • Seckel syndrome (ATR)
    • MOPD II (PCNT)
    • Nijmegen breakage syndrome (NBN)
    • LIG4 deficiency
    • Roberts syndrome (ESCO2), a cytogenetic look-alike of PCS

    Blind spots of a VCF-only analysis

    • Mobile-element insertions. One reported second BUB1B allele was an Alu insertion in intron 8, missed by coding-region screening. Short-variant VCFs do not represent such insertions. The child's FASTQ could be searched for them, but Phase 1 did not.
    • Regulatory variants beyond ±10 kb, other than the one listed known allele, were not scored.
    • Coverage gaps. Without reference blocks (gVCF) we cannot prove that every base of the panel was callable. The depth at called sites () is a proxy only.
    • Low-level mosaic variants in blood would be called poorly or not at all.

    None of these weakens the submitted pair; they bound what a negative result elsewhere can mean.

    Robustness of the ranking

    A ranking that survives only one exact setting of its weights is a fragile result, even when that setting was frozen in advance. Before running it, we registered a sensitivity protocol in config/phase2_protocol.yml (commit , ). The protocol was written after the first ranking run, when the top pair was already known. What it fixes is the set of perturbations: they were chosen systematically and committed before any of them was run, not picked after seeing which ones break the ranking. It changes one rule at a time:

    • each of the eleven point weights halved, doubled and removed
    • each frequency threshold tightened and relaxed
    • the SpliceAI and quality thresholds moved
    • each tie-break criterion moved to the front

    Each scenario re-runs route B's frozen code on a copy of the annotated tables and asks one question: is the baseline pair still first? No scenario can change the submission.

    Looking for the disease's signature in the child's own genome

    MVA is defined by a cellular phenotype, not a genotype. We asked whether that phenotype leaves a trace in the child's sequencing data, and stated beforehand what a negative answer would mean.

    Why it might, and why it probably will not

    Blood lymphocytes of children with PCS-MVA carry aneuploidy in roughly 9–32% of cells, involving almost every chromosome, with trisomies 7 and 8 relatively frequent. A mosaic gain of one chromosome in a fraction f of cells raises its read depth by f/2. It also shifts the allele balance at heterozygous sites away from one half, by f/(2(2+f)) for a gain and f/(2(2−f)) for a loss.

    Methods that combine allele balance and depth find clonal events above about 5–10% of cells from unphased arrays, and below 1% when haplotypes are phased. Gains are harder to detect than losses.

    MVA, however, scatters different aneuploidies across different cells, so any single chromosome may be abnormal in only a few percent of them. To our knowledge, no study has detected MVA from bulk array or sequencing data. One patient with 12% aneuploid cells on karyotype had a normal high-density SNP array. A negative result here was therefore the expected outcome, and its value lies in the bound it sets.

    Method, as registered

    From the normalised VCF we took PASS biallelic SNVs, dropping records split from multi-allelic sites, the pseudo-autosomal regions, and sites with depth below 10 or above 2.5 times the median. Two statistics were computed per chromosome:

    • Depth: the mean read depth relative to the autosomal mean.
    • Allele balance: the mean of z² at heterozygous sites, with z = (alt − n·p0) / √(n·p0(1−p0)). Here p0 is the genome-wide median alternate fraction, which absorbs reference bias.

    Each statistic was converted to a robust z-score against the autosomes (median and scaled MAD). The 22 autosomes were tested. The sex chromosomes were left out of the test and out of this public report.

    • Pre-specified test: gain of chromosome 7 or 8, called if both z-scores are at least 1.96.
    • Exploratory scan: any autosome with both z-scores at or beyond 3.5. Copy-neutral LOH is allele balance at or above 3.5 with |zdepth| below 2.

    The repository's tests run the scan on synthetic genomes: the pre-specified test finds a 15% mosaic gain of chromosome 7, the exploratory scan flags a 40% gain of chromosome 8, and a normal genome produces no call.

    Scalability: one engine, swappable disease knowledge

    The organisers ask whether an approach could “realistically be applied to other individuals, other diseases, or larger datasets”. We separated what is specific to MVA from what is not, wrote a one-command way to swap the specific part, and tested the result on public genomes under a pre-registered protocol.

    What is specific to MVA, and what is not

    Everything that decides how evidence is weighed is disease-agnostic: normalisation, annotation, the points scale, the frequency and quality thresholds, the pairing rule, the tie-breaks, the merge and the EPCR ladder. What is specific to MVA fits in two inputs:

    • three keys of ranking_rules.yml: the tier-A genes, the tier-B genes and the known non-coding alleles
    • the HPO terms given to route A

    scripts/make_rules.py writes a rules file for another disease by copying the frozen file and replacing only those three keys. Its input can be a curated gene list or a Genomics England PanelApp panel, whose green genes become tier A and amber genes tier B. A test checks that every other value is identical to the frozen rules. Route A needs no change, because Exomiser ranks every gene from whatever phenotype terms it is given. The aneuploidy scan works on any genome VCF, the report is generated from the results by code, and the data-boundary pattern (AI on code only, data steps as plain scripts) carries over to any controlled dataset.

    Other individuals and other diseases: a pre-registered benchmark

    The benchmark is part of the analysis protocol (Appendix , section benchmark), committed before it was run, and it uses public data only:

    • Seven real genomes. The Genome in a Bottle benchmark calls for HG001 to HG007 (v4.2.1, GRCh38): one individual of European ancestry, an Ashkenazi trio and a Han Chinese trio. Whatever recessive candidates route B finds in them unaided count as background.
    • Known pathogenic genotypes placed into them. Each draw takes pathogenic or likely pathogenic ClinVar variants with at least one review star, rare in gnomAD, in a recessive gene of the configuration. Three draws in four make a compound heterozygote and one in four a homozygote, with fifteen draws per genome and configuration.
    • Eight disease configurations, one engine. The MVA rules, and seven PanelApp panels at pinned versions, from cystic fibrosis (one gene) to intellectual disability (about 1,500 green genes). Route B's code and every weight were unchanged; make_rules.py swapped the gene lists.
    • Two readings of each genotype. Once with ClinVar annotation, as a positive control. Once with ClinVar evidence removed from the placed variants, as for newly found variants; this is the primary test.
    • Healthy genomes. Each genome was also ranked with nothing placed in it, to count the candidates a configuration produces by itself.

    What the benchmark does not show

    • Clinical sensitivity. It tests the engine and the gene-list swap, not how well any panel captures its disease.
    • Hard calls. The placed variants carry clean call metrics (depth 30, genotype quality 99). Real second alleles are often harder calls (Section ).
    • Splicing. SpliceAI was not run on the benchmark genomes, so non-canonical splice variants lose one route to qualify.
    • Related genomes. HG002 to HG004 and HG005 to HG007 are two parent–child trios, so the seven backgrounds share part of their variation and are not seven independent samples.
    • Difficult regions. GIAB benchmark calls cover high-confidence regions, so background noise from segmental duplications and other difficult regions is under-represented.
    • Route A. Exomiser was not re-run on the benchmark genomes (about half an hour each). Its performance across diseases is documented in large clinical evaluations.

    What the result means for the child

    A molecular diagnosis of MVA1 changes care now: it calls for confirmatory tests, tumour surveillance and family counselling. This section is written for the clinical team; it is not medical advice.

    Confirm the genotype and the cytogenetics

    • Confirm each variant by Sanger sequencing in a clinical laboratory.
    • Test both parents for the two variants. The expected result is one variant per parent, which establishes trans phase. Healthy parents of affected children can show total PCS in a minority of their lymphocytes (3.5–41.7% in one series), so a mild parental PCS finding is expected and is not a sign of disease.
    • Document the cytogenetic hallmark if it has not been documented: a karyotype scoring enough metaphases to estimate the aneuploid fraction, plus a PCS assessment. Orphanet bases the diagnosis on cytogenetic analysis showing variable aneuploidy.

    Settle the missense allele

    BUBR1 protein levels in the child's lymphoblastoid cells or fibroblasts are the natural assay. Expressed in cell lines, MVA1 missense proteins with substitutions in or near the kinase domain reach 5- to 10-fold lower levels than wild type, and the two tested for it, I909T and L1012P, are lost when HSP90 is inhibited. Two experiments would supply PS3-level evidence for p.(Asn1002Lys):

    • reduced BUBR1 in patient cells
    • failure of the Asn1002Lys protein to rescue BUBR1-depleted cells

    Tumour surveillance

    About a third of people with MVA develop cancer, almost always before age five. In MVA1 the dominant risk is Wilms tumour, reported in about half of patients with BUB1B variants. Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma and leukaemia also occur.

    The SIOP-Europe Host Genome Working Group recommends Wilms tumour surveillance for every patient with cytogenetically confirmed MVA: renal ultrasound every three months until the seventh birthday. The AACR recommendations apply the same schedule to any condition with a Wilms tumour risk of at least 1%. We found no guideline specific to rhabdomyosarcoma or leukaemia in MVA; clinical vigilance is the current standard.

    Genetic counselling

    MVA1 is autosomal recessive. If trans phase is confirmed, each parent is a carrier and each future pregnancy has a 25% recurrence risk. Carrier testing of relatives and prenatal or preimplantation testing become possible once both variants are confirmed.

    Research value

    The genotype pairs a null allele with a missense allele that is predicted, but not yet shown, to destabilise the protein. If the assay confirms it, the child's BUBR1 level is set mainly by how well the Asn1002Lys protein folds, and HSP90 dependence suggests that folding could be modulated. Track 2 of this hackathon builds on that mechanism.

    Reproducibility

    The repository () contains every script, the frozen rules, the analysis protocol and the environment records. It also contains automated tests that run on synthetic data in about a minute and cover:

    • rule scoring and tie-breaks
    • cis exclusion and multi-allelic splits
    • known-allele handling and gene aliases
    • CSV assembly and the scorer interface
    • the pre-registration gates, masking and downloads
    • macOS shell compatibility

    Limitations

    • Phase is inferred, not observed. The pair is presumed in trans on biological grounds; parental testing is needed.
    • The missense allele is a VUS today. Its classification depends on phase and on a functional assay that has not been done.
    • Route B is disease-specific by design. Its panel encodes the known diagnosis, and route A is the gene-agnostic check. Pre-registration controls tuning, not prior knowledge.
    • The points system is a heuristic. Route B's points follow the ACMG/AMP Bayesian scale, but they rank; they do not classify. The classifications in Section were made separately and conservatively.
    • Some allele types are invisible here. Mobile-element insertions, distant regulatory variants and low-level mosaic variants cannot be seen in this VCF.
    • The aneuploidy scan is unphased and bulk. Its stated detection limit is far above the per-chromosome fractions expected in MVA.
    • The computational predictors disagree for the missense allele, and AlphaMissense is known to miss some destabilising variants in this domain.
    • Route B's reach depends on its gene list. The benchmark (Section ) measures how the frozen engine carries over to other diseases and how many biallelic candidates a healthy genome yields as the panel grows. Route B is therefore read together with the phenotype-driven route A, which needs no gene list.
